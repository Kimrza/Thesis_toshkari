- ALWAYS split raised assumptions into verification obligations this project must check and governance dependencies owned outside it, rather than one flat assumptions list. (learned 2026-08-13) <!-- cid:intent-capture:c3 -->
- ALWAYS honour a human's disposition by narrowing what a rule requires, never by relocating a rule that the governing normative core fixes; a stage answer cannot move a requirement out of the layer the authority document places it in. (learned 2026-08-13) <!-- cid:intent-capture:c7 -->
- ALWAYS check the drafted artifact against the governing normative core before the approval gate, not only the questions that produced it; contradictions between an artifact and the authority document are invisible to question-level checking. (learned 2026-08-13) <!-- cid:intent-capture:c4 -->
- ALWAYS raise a targeted follow-up when an answer would require building something before the work that specifies its contents; reinterpreting such an answer silently hides an ordering error that only surfaces once the wrong thing is built. (learned 2026-08-15) <!-- cid:approval-handoff:c3 -->
- ALWAYS enumerate every open supervisor gate from the governing authority's gate table in a phase handoff, not only the gates on the visible critical path; a partial list makes the unlisted gates invisible to the receiving phase. (learned 2026-08-15) <!-- cid:approval-handoff:c1 -->
- ALWAYS verify a fact independently before handing it to another reviewer as established input; a wrong fact promoted to established status is built upon rather than questioned, and the reviewer's conclusion inherits the error. (learned 2026-08-16) <!-- cid:practices-discovery:c-board-1 -->
- ALWAYS derive a count programmatically from the artifact and print it before asserting it; never carry a count from adjacent prose, from a finding's text, or from an earlier revision. Three counts asserted this way in one session were all wrong (a release-manifest field count, an untested-requirement list size, and a test-module count claimed to appear in four places when it appears in two), while every count derived from the artifact was right. (learned 2026-08-21) <!-- cid:application-design:application-design:count-derivation -->
- ALWAYS sweep for the superlatives and status claims an amended figure supported, not only for the superseded numeral. A sweep keyed to the old number is structurally blind to a stale superlative ("the largest untested share of any unit") and to a stale claim carrying no numeral ("none of the four has a §19 acceptance row"), and four of the six defects found on resume at delivery-planning were of those two kinds. `governance/CHANGE_RECORD_PROCEDURE.md` step 2 inherits the same blind spot in the words "sweep the workspace for that literal". (learned 2026-08-22) <!-- cid:delivery-planning:c22 -->
- ALWAYS reconcile two artifacts that should agree on a count by set-differencing their ID lists, never by comparing their totals. A total mismatch proves a disagreement exists and says nothing about which side is wrong; the set difference names the culprits. A first count of the untested requirements read 40 against the story map's 36, and only the set difference showed the four extra IDs were crosswalk range-leads picked up by my own regex rather than a defect in either artifact. Two lists of equal length can also still disagree, which a total can never reveal. (learned 2026-08-22) <!-- cid:delivery-planning:c21 -->
- NEVER reopen an earlier recorded refusal merely because the material it refused is now within reach. Reversal needs a new argument or an explicit human decision, and the original reasoning must be honoured rather than quietly dropped. When `CR-2026-08-22-INC-CORRECTIONS` refused to reconstruct a lost governance report as circular self-evidence, having the change records in hand later did not make that reasoning wrong; the owner's fallback changed the artifact's label and stated the circularity as a limit, rather than defeating the refusal. (learned 2026-08-22) <!-- cid:delivery-planning:c12 -->
- ALWAYS verify every item of a proposed fix scope at its named location in the artifact before putting that scope to the human for a ruling. A scope assembled from a review's findings list describes what was true when the review ran, not what is on disk now: twelve items were presented as outstanding and seven were already implemented, so the ruling was given on a scope that misdescribed the work. Where the two differ, state the correction before acting on the ruling rather than quietly shipping the smaller set. (learned 2026-08-23) <!-- cid:application-design:c5 -->
- ALWAYS sweep every REPRESENTATION of a corrected fact, not every instance of the entity that carries it. A register entry, the owning unit's own paragraph, a summary table and a roll-up row are four representations of one fact, and correcting the entry alone leaves the other three asserting the superseded version to exactly the reader they were written for. BLK-04's mechanism was rewritten in the register while `features-and-splits`'s own Blockers paragraph still asserted the rejected containment rule as current fact, and BLK-08/BLK-09 reached no per-unit paragraph at all - which defeated the stated reason for registering them as blockers rather than residual notes. (learned 2026-08-23) <!-- cid:units-generation:re-1 -->
- ALWAYS write stage-diary entries under the four standard headings exactly - Interpretations, Deviations, Tradeoffs, Open questions. The learnings ritual surfaces candidates by matching those headings, so a qualified heading such as '## Deviations (2026-08-23 re-entry)' is kept on disk but invisible to the ritual that exists to read it: a re-entry's three new tradeoffs and one new deviation surfaced as zero candidates while four superseded entries surfaced instead. (learned 2026-08-23) <!-- cid:units-generation:re-2 -->
- ALWAYS extend a correction sweep into the downstream artifacts that consumed the corrected fact, not only the artifact that stated it. Each stage sweeping its own outputs leaves the boundary between them unswept: units-generation corrected two acceptance-row figures on 2026-08-22 and delivery-planning's Bolt table, written from the pre-correction values, still carried them two days later. Where the consuming stage declares no reviewer, that sweep is the only check standing between the stale figure and Construction. (learned 2026-08-24) <!-- cid:delivery-planning:dp-1 -->

- ALWAYS sweep for a corrected figure in BOTH its spelled-out and numeral forms, and across every surface a reader meets - Mermaid diagram node labels, table cells, section headings, change-log and remediation boxes, Sources citations, gate-item and routing lists, and the artifact's own requirement-coverage table - not only its prose and its `## Assumptions & Open Questions` bullets. A `governance-guards` repair verified itself with a grep for the word "four" and so was structurally blind to a diagram node reading `(4 members, asserted exactly)`, which sat above the correction box that was supposed to fix it. Three separate units this stage had the corrected fact land in `## Assumptions` while a rule's own Rule statement or an entity's own field table - what an implementer reads first - kept asserting the superseded version. (learned 2026-08-30) <!-- cid:functional-design:fd-2026-08-30-sweep-numerals-and-surfaces -->
- NEVER edit a human-signed record - a receipted Consolidated Summary Confirmation, an approved decision, a completed-stage artifact - to bring it into line with a later derivation. Record the correction in the artifacts you own, state the derived value with its derivation printed, and route ONE explicit ruling to the human: annotate the signed record in place, or carry the correction in the gate record alone. `governance/CHANGE_RECORD_PROCEDURE.md` permits annotate-in-place only with owner approval for the specific item, so editing a signature to match a recount is exactly what that procedure exists to prevent. When `features-and-splits`'s signed summary claimed a "nine-site sweep" that a printed grep showed to be three sites, the correction went into the unit's own Open items and the ruling to the gate; the signed text was left standing.
